What your technicians capture on site is what gets your invoices approved without questions. OpenWrench makes the documentation part of the workflow instead of an afterthought.

## Field workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check in on arrival">
    Technicians check in when they arrive, creating a timestamped record of time on site. Customers see that you showed up — within the window you committed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document the diagnosis">
    Photos of the problem, notes on cause. If the job needs more than the NTE covers, submit the [NTE increase request](/suppliers/quotes) right from the work order with that evidence attached.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log the work">
    Work performed, parts used, and any follow-up needed. Work logs are per service call, so multi-visit jobs stay organized.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check out and close the visit">
    Completion notes and after photos close the service call. If the job needs a return visit, say so — the work order stays open with the next step visible.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why the evidence matters to you

- **Invoices sail through** when billed hours match check-in/check-out times and after-photos show completed work.
- **Disputes end quickly** when the record shows what you found and what you did.
- **First-time-fix rates** — a KPI customers watch — are only credible when visits are documented.

<Tip>
  Before-and-after photos are the single highest-value habit for a field tech on this platform. Thirty seconds of photos routinely saves thirty minutes of invoice questions.
</Tip>

## Troubleshooting support

For some problem types, the work order includes the troubleshooting steps site staff already walked through before dispatch — so your tech arrives knowing what's been ruled out.
