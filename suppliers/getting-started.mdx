When a customer invites you to their OpenWrench network, a few setup steps get you from invited to receiving work.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your account and company profile">
    Accept the invitation and fill in your company details — contacts, service address, and the basics customers see about you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Declare your trades">
    Pick your trades and sub-trades from OpenWrench's trade taxonomy — plumbing, electrical, HVAC, refrigeration, and the finer specialties within each. Trades determine which work orders can route to you, so declare everything you genuinely cover and nothing you don't.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your coverage">
    Define which of the customer's locations or geographic areas you serve. Coverage plus trades is how dispatchers (and automated dispatch) find you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload compliance documents">
    Insurance certificates, licenses, and whatever your customer requires. Expirations are tracked — you'll be prompted before documents lapse, because lapsed compliance can pause you from receiving dispatches.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your team">
    Invite office staff and field technicians with appropriate roles. Field techs get a mobile-friendly view for on-site workflows; office staff handle scheduling, quoting, and invoicing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Notifications

Configure how your team hears about new work — who gets notified for new dispatches, schedule changes, approval decisions, and payment updates. Getting this right on day one is the difference between a 10-minute acceptance time and missing work because it sat unseen.

<Tip>
  Customers can see responsiveness. Accepting work quickly — even to say "scheduled for Thursday" — is the single most visible thing you control in your performance record.
</Tip>

## Serving multiple customers

If several of your customers run on OpenWrench, they all flow through the same supplier account — one login, one team setup, work from every customer in one queue.
