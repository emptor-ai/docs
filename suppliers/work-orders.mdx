Work orders from your OpenWrench customers land in your queue with the context you actually need — not a two-line email.

## What arrives with a work order

- **The problem** — description, problem type, priority, and photos or video from the site
- **The asset** — make, model, serial, and its service history when the work is equipment-related
- **The location** — address, hours, access notes, and who to ask for on site
- **The NTE** — the amount you're authorized to spend without further approval

## Accepting (or declining) work

Review and respond quickly — acceptance time is one of the most visible things in your performance record. If you can't take a job (outside your coverage, booked out, wrong trade), decline promptly so the customer can reassign. A fast decline costs you nothing; a slow one costs the customer downtime and you goodwill.

## Scheduling and service calls

Each visit against a work order is a **service call** with its own schedule and ETA:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Commit a service window">
    Schedule the visit. The location sees it, so on-site staff expect you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep your ETA honest">
    Update the ETA as the visit approaches. Slipping silently is the fastest way to hurt your reliability record; updating early keeps everyone working with you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reschedule in the open">
    Parts delays and emergencies happen. Reschedule on the work order with a note — the record protects you when the story is documented.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Communication in one place

Questions about scope, access, or parts go in the work order thread — not to someone's personal email. Everything you write is part of the job record, which means decisions you were given in writing stay findable when it matters (like invoice review).

<Note>
  Multi-visit jobs are normal: a diagnostic service call, then a return with parts. Open additional service calls rather than stretching one — each visit's time and work stays cleanly documented, which keeps your invoicing clean too.
</Note>
