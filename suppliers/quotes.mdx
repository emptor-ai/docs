Every dispatch carries an **NTE (Not-To-Exceed)** — the amount you can spend without further approval. When a job fits under it, just do the work. When it doesn't, two paths get you authorized: an NTE increase request or a quote.

## NTE increase requests

For jobs where diagnosis reveals more than the NTE covers, submit an **NTE increase request** from the work order:

1. State the new amount you need and why.
2. Attach evidence — photos of what you found, the failed part, the extra scope.
3. The request routes to the customer's approvers automatically. You'll see the decision on the work order.

<Tip>
  Requests with photos and a specific number get approved dramatically faster than "it's going to be more, call me." The approver on the other end can say yes in one click when your justification is in front of them.
</Tip>

## Quotes

For larger or defined-scope work — replacements, projects, anything the customer wants to see priced before committing — submit a **quote** on the work order:

- **Line items** for labor, parts, and materials
- **Scope description** — what's included and what isn't
- **Attachments** — spec sheets, options, photos

Approved quotes become the agreed budget the job (and your invoice) is measured against. If the customer has questions, the conversation happens in the work order thread, and the final agreement is on the record.

## Bidding on sourcing events

Customers sometimes run structured RFPs (**sourcing events**) for contracts, rollouts, or re-bids. You'll be invited, see the scope, and submit your bid in the platform. Winning a sourcing event typically means recurring work across the customer's locations.

## What not to do

Don't perform beyond-NTE work on a verbal OK. The approval trail on the work order is what guarantees your invoice matches an authorization — protecting you at payment time.
