Invoicing in OpenWrench is invoicing against the record: the work order already holds the NTE or approved quote, your check-in/check-out times, work logs, and completion photos. An invoice that matches that record approves quickly.

## Submitting an invoice

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invoice from the work order">
    Create the invoice on the completed work order with line items for labor, parts, and materials. Parts logged during service calls can flow straight into the invoice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stay inside the authorization">
    Bill within the NTE or approved quote. Amounts above what was authorized will bounce back — if scope grew, that conversation should have happened as an NTE increase before the work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit and track">
    Watch status move through review and approval to payment — visible to you the whole way.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## If an invoice is questioned

Disputes happen on the work order thread, where both sides see the same evidence. This is where field documentation pays off: check-in/check-out timestamps back your labor hours, and after-photos back the work. Respond with specifics and the record usually settles it.

## Getting paid

Approved invoices flow into the customer's payment process, with status visible throughout — no calls to accounts payable to find out where things stand. Payment timing follows your terms with each customer.

<Note>
  OpenWrench takes no cut of your invoices. What you bill and what the customer approves is between you and them — the platform just makes the process visible to both sides.
</Note>
