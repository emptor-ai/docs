Reactive repair is what everyone sees; preventive maintenance is what determines how much of it you need. OpenWrench runs PM as a first-class program, not a spreadsheet on the side.

## PM programs

A **PM program** defines recurring maintenance — what gets done, where, how often, and by whom:

- **Scope** — the tasks to perform, tied to asset types or specific assets (quarterly HVAC service, monthly grease trap cleaning, annual fire inspection)
- **Schedule** — the recurrence that generates work orders automatically, with lead time for scheduling
- **Assignee** — a network supplier or your internal team, per location or region

Generated PM work orders flow through the same lifecycle as reactive work — dispatch, service calls, completion evidence, invoicing — so PM history lives on the same assets and in the same reporting as everything else.

## PM compliance

Scheduling PMs is easy; the hard part is knowing they actually happened. **PM compliance** tracking shows, by location, region, and program:

- What was due, completed on time, completed late, or missed
- Which locations and which suppliers are consistently behind
- Compliance trends over time

<Note>
  For teams with regulatory or brand-standard obligations — healthcare facilities, food service, franchise brands — compliance reporting doubles as your audit evidence: due dates, completion records, and technician documentation in one place.
</Note>

## Making PM stick

- **Tie PMs to assets**, not just locations — that's what builds the maintenance history behind repair-vs-replace calls.
- **Require completion evidence** (photos, checklist confirmation) so "done" means done.
- **Watch the reactive/preventive mix** in reporting. Locations with slipping PM compliance and rising emergency work orders are telling you something.
- **Let VFA chase the stragglers** — [VFA PM support](/buyers/vfa) follows up on overdue PMs so your team doesn't have to.
