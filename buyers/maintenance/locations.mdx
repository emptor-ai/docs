Everything in OpenWrench happens somewhere. The location hierarchy is what turns hundreds of sites into an organized portfolio.

## The hierarchy

- **Locations** are individual sites — a restaurant, a gym, a clinic, a store. Each carries its address, hours, contacts, documents, and settings.
- **Regions** (and sub-regions) group locations for management and reporting — typically mirroring your operational structure: districts, markets, territories.
- **Areas** describe places _within_ a location — kitchen, dining room, roof, mechanical room — and can nest. Work orders tagged to areas tell technicians where to go and let reporting spot patterns ("roof leaks cluster in the northeast sites").

## What lives on a location

- **Contacts** — who to reach on site, who gets notified about work
- **Documents** — leases, floor plans, permits, equipment lists
- **Assets** — the equipment installed at the site
- **Access notes** — hours, alarm procedures, parking, loading dock details that save suppliers a wasted trip
- **Audit history** — changes to the location record over time

## Regions drive visibility and rollups

The region tree does double duty:

- **Permissions** — regional managers see their regions; location staff see their site
- **Reporting** — spend, work order volume, PM compliance, and supplier performance all roll up the same tree, so a portfolio number can always be decomposed to the sites behind it

<Tip>
  Mirror your actual org chart in the region structure. When the region tree matches how responsibility really flows, every report lands on someone's desk who can act on it.
</Tip>
