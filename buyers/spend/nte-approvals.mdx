The **Not-To-Exceed (NTE)** limit is OpenWrench's core cost control. Every dispatch carries a maximum the supplier may spend without coming back for approval — fast enough for routine work, safe enough for expensive surprises.

## How NTEs work

1. A work order is dispatched with an NTE — set per work order, or defaulted by problem type or location policy.
2. The supplier can perform and bill work up to that amount without further sign-off.
3. If diagnosis reveals the job costs more, the supplier submits an **NTE increase request** from the work order, with justification.
4. The request routes through your **approval hierarchy** and, once approved, the supplier continues — no phone tag, no forwarded email chains.

## Approval hierarchies

Approval hierarchies define who signs off at what threshold. A typical setup:

| Amount | Approver |
| --- | --- |
| Up to the default NTE | No approval needed |
| Above NTE, under a set threshold | Facilities manager |
| Larger amounts | Director / regional leader |
| Major expenditures | Finance / executive approval |

Hierarchies are configurable per organization — by amount, spend category, region, or approval type — and the same machinery powers quote approvals, purchase request approvals for internal teams, and invoice approvals.

## Reviewing an NTE increase request

When an increase request reaches you, the work order gives you what you need to decide quickly:

- The supplier's **justification and photos** from on site
- The **asset's service history** — is this the third repair this year?
- **Historical costs** for similar work, so you can judge whether the number is reasonable

<Tip>
  A fast "no" is more valuable than a slow "yes." Suppliers on site waiting for approval are burning time — and repeat slow approvals show up in how suppliers prioritize your locations. VFA NTE oversight can watch pending requests and nudge approvers so requests don't sit.
</Tip>

## Where NTE decisions show up later

Every NTE, increase request, and approval decision is part of the work order's permanent record. Invoice review checks billed amounts against approved NTEs, and spend reporting rolls approved amounts into your budget picture — so the guardrail at dispatch time becomes the audit trail at review time.
