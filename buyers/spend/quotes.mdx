For work beyond a simple repair — replacements, projects, anything where you want to see a number before committing — suppliers submit **quotes** (proposals) on the work order for your review.

## The quote flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Supplier submits a quote">
    After a diagnostic visit or from the job description, the supplier submits a quote with line items, scope, and attachments directly on the work order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You review in context">
    The quote sits next to the problem description, photos, asset history, and past costs for similar work — everything needed to judge scope and price.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve, decline, or negotiate">
    Approval routes through your approval hierarchy based on amount. Questions and negotiation happen in the work order thread, so the record stays complete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approved quote becomes the job's budget">
    Once approved, the quote sets the expectation the eventual invoice is checked against.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Comparing multiple quotes

For significant work you can collect quotes from multiple suppliers on the same work order, or run a structured **sourcing event (RFP)** across your network — useful for rollouts, capital projects, and annual contract work. See [Managing Suppliers](/buyers/suppliers/managing-suppliers) for sourcing events.

## Repair or replace?

Quote review is where repair-vs-replace decisions get made. OpenWrench puts the asset's full history on the table: what it has cost to date, how often it fails, and how this quote compares. A $$1,900 compressor repair reads differently when the record shows $$4,300 of repairs on that unit in eighteen months.

<Note>
  Quotes, NTEs, and invoices are complementary controls: NTEs govern spend without pre-approval, quotes pre-approve defined scopes, and invoice review verifies what was billed matches what was approved.
</Note>
