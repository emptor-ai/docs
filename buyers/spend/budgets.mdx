Spend control isn't only per-work-order guardrails — it's knowing where the money goes in aggregate. Two structures make that work.

## Spend categories

Every work order and invoice maps to a **spend category** — your organization's taxonomy for maintenance spend (repairs and maintenance, capital expenditure, janitorial, landscaping, and whatever divisions your finance team reports by). Categories keep OpenWrench's numbers reconcilable with your accounting structure, and let reporting slice spend the way finance actually talks about it.

## Budgets

**Budgets** set expected spend by location, region, or category over a period. As approved invoices accumulate, actuals track against budget — so overruns surface mid-period while there's still time to react, not at quarter close.

## Estimated spend, before the money is spent

Because OpenWrench holds your historical costs by problem type and trade, it can show an **estimated cost range at work order creation** — grounded in your own invoice history, not industry averages. Estimates sharpen as the work order progresses: a historical estimate gives way to the quote, which gives way to the approved invoice.

That means your committed-spend picture includes work that's still in flight — not just what's been invoiced.

<Tip>
  Estimated spend also makes NTE-setting less of a guessing game: if the typical walk-in cooler repair at your locations runs $$600–$$900, an NTE of \$750 is a policy, not a hunch.
</Tip>
