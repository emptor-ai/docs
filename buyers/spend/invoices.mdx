When work completes, the supplier submits an invoice **on the work order itself** — which changes what invoice review means. Instead of a PDF in an inbox and a hunt for context, the invoice arrives attached to the complete record of what was approved and what actually happened.

## Reviewing an invoice

Every invoice is checked against the work order it bills:

- **Against the NTE or approved quote** — is the amount within what was authorized?
- **Against the work logs** — do billed hours match time on site?
- **Against completion evidence** — closing notes and photos confirm the work was done

Discrepancies get raised in the work order thread, where the supplier can respond with context — disputes resolve faster when both sides look at the same record.

## Approval and payment

Invoice approval routes through your approval hierarchy like any other spend decision. Approved invoices flow into your payment process, and every step — submitted, under review, approved, paid — is visible to the supplier too, which cuts "where's my payment?" calls dramatically.

<Note>
  Suppliers see invoice status in their own portal at no charge to them. Predictable, transparent payment is a big part of why good suppliers prioritize OpenWrench customers' work.
</Note>

## Invoice monitoring

At portfolio scale, invoice problems are pattern problems: a supplier who consistently bills at 100% of NTE, invoices that arrive months late, line items that creep. [VFA invoice monitoring](/buyers/vfa) watches for these patterns across your account and flags what deserves human attention.

## Spend visibility

Approved invoices are the ground truth for spend reporting — by location, region, spend category, trade, supplier, or asset. That's what makes questions like "what did refrigeration cost us last quarter, and which locations drove it?" answerable in minutes in [Reporting](/buyers/reporting/dashboards).
