Everything that happens in OpenWrench — every work order, service call, approval, and invoice — becomes reportable the moment it happens. Reporting turns that operational record into answers.

## Dashboards

Role-appropriate dashboards give each audience its daily picture:

- **Facilities teams** — open work orders, aging, stuck jobs, pending approvals, today's scheduled visits
- **Regional leaders** — volume, spend, and PM compliance rolled up by region, with drill-down to the site
- **Finance** — spend against budget by category, invoice pipeline, committed spend still in flight

An **attention dashboard** view keeps the exceptions in front of you: ETAs slipping, approvals sitting, work orders going quiet — the things that need a human today.

## KPIs worth watching

| KPI | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Work order volume & aging | Demand and whether you're keeping up |
| Time to dispatch / time to resolve | Where the process is slow |
| Reactive vs. preventive mix | Whether PM investment is paying off |
| PM compliance | Which locations and programs are slipping |
| Spend by category / region / supplier | Where the money goes, and outliers |
| Estimated vs. actual cost | How predictable your spend is becoming |

## Custom reports

Beyond built-in views, **custom reports** let you define the slice you need — filters, columns, and groupings across work order, spend, asset, and supplier data — and export or schedule it. CSV downloads are available throughout for anything your finance or BI team wants to take elsewhere.

## Savings you can point to

OpenWrench also quantifies the money that _didn't_ get spent: work orders deflected by troubleshooting flows, warranty-covered repairs caught before payment, and invoice reductions from NTE and quote discipline. Those numbers are the fastest way to show what the program is worth.
