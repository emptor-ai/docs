Once a work order exists, it needs the right hands. OpenWrench supports three assignment paths — and most organizations use all three.

## Assign to your private network

The most common path: dispatch to a supplier from your **private network**. OpenWrench surfaces suppliers that match the work order's trade and the location's coverage area, along with their performance history, so dispatchers choose with real information instead of habit.

## Assign to internal teams

Work that your own technicians handle — handyman work, PM routes, minor repairs — dispatches to **internal teams** instead of a third party. Internal techs get the same work order context and use [WrenchMode](/internal-teams/wrenchmode) to check in and out on site. Deciding what stays in-house versus what goes out is one of the biggest cost levers the platform gives you.

## Let the Virtual Facilities Assistant dispatch

With [VFA dispatch](/buyers/vfa), OpenWrench's managed operational layer handles assignment for you: selecting the supplier, confirming acceptance, and following up until the job is scheduled. Your team sets the rules and guardrails (NTEs, preferred suppliers, escalation criteria); VFA does the legwork.

## What happens after assignment

<Steps>
  <Step title="Supplier accepts the work order">
    The supplier sees the full context — problem, photos, asset history, NTE — and accepts or declines. Declines route back for reassignment fast.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Visit gets scheduled">
    The supplier commits to a service window, visible on the work order and to the location.
  </Step>
  <Step title="ETA confirmed">
    As the visit approaches, ETAs keep everyone honest — slipping ETAs are visible and chase-able.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Emergencies

For urgent, after-hours issues — flooding, no heat, walk-in cooler failure — emergency dispatch shortcuts the normal cadence: higher-priority routing, immediate supplier outreach, and escalation if the first supplier can't respond. Emergency work orders still carry NTEs and land in the same reporting as everything else.

<Tip>
  Keep NTEs realistic for emergency problem types. An NTE that's too low guarantees a mid-job approval scramble at 2am; the approval hierarchy still protects you from surprises.
</Tip>
