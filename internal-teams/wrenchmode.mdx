**WrenchMode** is the internal technician's field companion: check in when you start a job, check out when you're done, and let the timeline build itself. No timesheets to reconstruct on Friday afternoon.

## How it works

- **Check in / check out** — each job visit is bracketed by check-in and check-out events, producing **true work time** per service call.
- **Drive time** — time between jobs is captured as drive time, separate from wrench time. If something's off (a lunch stop, a forgotten checkout), drive time can be revised with the correction on the record.
- **Schedules** — WrenchMode schedules define which locations a technician covers and when, so the day's route is planned rather than improvised.

## Why techs come out ahead

Time tracking usually feels like surveillance. WrenchMode's record mostly works in the technician's favor:

- Your day is **self-documenting** — nobody asks what you did Tuesday; the timeline shows six jobs and the drive between them.
- **Wrench time vs. windshield time** becomes visible — the case for better routing, territory changes, or another hire is in the data, not in complaints.
- Job time feeds the true cost of in-house work, which is how the organization justifies keeping (and growing) the internal team.

## For managers

WrenchMode analytics summarize time across technicians and date ranges: work time, drive time, jobs per day, and how the mix trends. Use it to balance routes and workloads — and to catch the tech who's drowning before they burn out, not after.

<Tip>
  Make checkout part of leaving the site, like loading the truck. Revising a forgotten checkout later is possible, but a clean live record beats a reconstructed one every time.
</Tip>
