PM is where internal teams shine: predictable, schedulable work that keeps techs productive between emergencies — and keeps the emergencies from happening.

## How PM reaches you

PM programs assigned to internal teams generate work orders on schedule, landing in your queue with lead time. Each PM work order carries its checklist — the tasks that define "done" for that visit — tied to the assets being serviced.

## Working PM efficiently

- **Route by geography** — WrenchMode schedules let PM visits cluster by location and day, so a PM day is a loop, not a scatter.
- **Pair PM with the punch list** — you're already on site; knock out the open minor work orders at that location in the same visit. Dispatchers can see what's open where you're headed.
- **Complete the checklist honestly** — a skipped filter marked done is worse than a skipped filter marked skipped. Compliance reporting is only useful when it's true.
- **Flag what you find** — PM visits are inspection opportunities. The belt that's cracking but not broken becomes a planned work order today instead of an emergency in March.

## Compliance is the scoreboard

The organization tracks **PM compliance** — due vs. done, on time vs. late — by location and program. For internal teams, that number is effectively the team's report card, and it's the argument for headcount when routes grow.

<Note>
  Findings from PM visits that turn into planned work orders are the clearest evidence PM is working: every one is an emergency that didn't happen.
</Note>
