Internal technicians don't invoice — they buy. **Purchase requests (PRs)** are how parts, materials, and supplies get approved and purchased, with the same approval machinery that governs the rest of the organization's spend.

## The flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Raise the request">
    From the job (or standalone for stock replenishment), request what you need: items, quantities, estimated cost, and the work order it's for. Line items keep it concrete.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approval routes automatically">
    The PR flows through the organization's approval hierarchy based on amount and category. Small routine purchases clear fast; bigger buys reach the right approver without you chasing anyone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buy and record">
    Once approved, purchase — through a purchase order to a vendor, or directly per your organization's process — and the actual cost lands on the record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cost lands on the job">
    Parts bought for a work order attach to it, so the true cost of in-house work — labor from WrenchMode plus materials from PRs — stays complete.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Spot purchases

Some organizations enable **spot purchase** flows for the supply-house run — the $$40 fitting you need right now. Same trail, lighter process, so policy compliance doesn't cost you an hour on a $$40 part.

## Why the trail matters

PR history answers questions that otherwise turn into arguments: what did we spend on materials by location last quarter, which parts do we buy repeatedly (and should stock instead), and did that job's cost stay reasonable once labor and parts are added up.

<Tip>
  Tie every job-related purchase to its work order, even when it feels like overhead. "Materials, unattached, \$6,300 this quarter" is a finance headache; the same spend attached to jobs is just the cost of doing maintenance.
</Tip>
