Internal teams carry inventory — shop stock, truck stock, and the parts cage at bigger sites. OpenWrench tracks what's where and what got used on which job.

## The pieces

- **Stock locations** — anywhere parts live: the shop, a warehouse, each truck, a site's storage room. Locations can be linked to the sites they serve.
- **Parts catalog** — the items you stock, with units of measure and costs.
- **Usage against jobs** — parts consumed on a service call come out of stock and onto the work order's cost record.
- **Replenishment** — stock running low feeds [purchase requests](/internal-teams/purchase-requests) to bring it back up.

## Truck stock, honestly

The truck is where inventory systems usually die. Two habits keep it alive:

1. **Log usage at checkout** — while checking out of a job in WrenchMode, the parts you used are fresh in mind. Next week they're gone.
2. **Count the truck on a cadence** — a monthly fifteen-minute true-up beats an annual archaeology project.

## What the data buys the team

- **Right parts on the truck** — usage history shows what each territory actually burns, so trucks carry what their routes need.
- **Fewer second trips** — the most expensive part is the one you have to drive back for.
- **Real job costing** — materials tracked to jobs completes the picture WrenchMode starts, which is the number the in-house-vs-outsource decision runs on.
